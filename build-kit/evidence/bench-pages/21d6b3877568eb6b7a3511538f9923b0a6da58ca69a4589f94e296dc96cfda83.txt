**Warning: Text Scam Reports**

We have had reports that individuals have been engaging in a caller ID spoofing scam, using Seattle Municipal Court phone numbers, and scams involving text messages, some with QR codes. No one from SMC will call you and ask for your personal information or request money. If you should get one of these calls, please hang up and try calling the number displayed in the caller ID to see where the call is coming from. The FCC website (https://www.fcc.gov/spoofing) is a great resource to learn more about caller ID spoofing, including how to file a complaint if you think you have become a victim of a spoofing scam.

Dispute My Ticket

Ways to Respond

You can respond to a Notice of Infraction in four ways:

Pay the full amount.

Ask the court about a payment plan.

Ask for a Mitigation Hearing.

Ask for a Contested Hearing.

Pay Your Ticket

To pay in full, visit Pay My Ticket.

If you cannot pay in full, you may ask for a payment plan. The court may charge a fee.

To learn more, visit I Can’t Pay My Ticket Right Now.

Ask for a Hearing

If you want to dispute your ticket, you must ask for a hearing.

You must ask within:

30 days if the ticket was issued to you or placed on your car.

33 days if the ticket was mailed to you.

How to Request a Hearing

You can request a hearing in three ways:

1. Online form.

Fill out the Hearing Request Form online. This is the easiest option if you have internet access.

2. Use Your Ticket.

On the back of your ticket:

Check the box for the hearing you want.

Write in your information.

Bring it to 600 5th Avenue, or mail it to:

SEATTLE MUNICIPAL COURT

PO BOX 34987

SEATTLE, WA 98124-4987

3. Call the Court

Call (206) 684-5600.

Monday–Tuesday: 8 AM-5 PM

Wednesday–Friday: 9 AM-5 PM

Tell the representative your ticket number and the hearing type you want.

The court will mail or email you the date and time of your hearing.

Hearing Types

Mitigation Hearing

Choose this if you agree you broke the rule but want to explain your situation.

The decision is final. You cannot appeal.

Contested Hearing

Choose this if you believe you did not break the rule.

You will first get a pre‑hearing settlement conference.

This is a chance to resolve the issue without a full hearing.

You may skip this step the pre-hearing and go to a trial instead. To do that, fill out the waiver form sent with your notice.

If you contest your ticket in writing, you cannot appeal. The decision is final.

Send Your Evidence

If you want the judge to see photos or documents:

Upload them to the Public Portal, or

Email them to CourtCalendar.SMC@seattle.gov.
